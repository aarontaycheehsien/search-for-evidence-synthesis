# Pilot: guide for participants

Thank you for helping to test *How Search Finds and Misses Studies: Information Retrieval for Evidence Synthesis*. This guide says what to read, what to try and how to tell us what went wrong.

## What the book is for

The book is for librarians and information specialists who already build structured searches for systematic, scoping and rapid reviews. It explains how search systems decide which records a review team ever sees, so that readers can diagnose where an eligible study was lost and judge a proposed search tool against the claim actually made for it.

It is a working draft. We want to find where it fails, so critical comments are the most useful kind.

## What we ask you to do

About five hours of reading and exercises over three weeks, two short checks of 15 minutes each, and a one-hour conversation at the end.

| When | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Before you start | Answer the short knowledge check (part A) we send you, without looking anything up | 15 minutes |
| Week 1 | Read Chapter 1. Attempt Exercises 1.1 and 1.2 | About 1.5 hours |
| Week 2 | Read Chapter 3. Attempt Exercises 3.1 and 3.2 | About 1.5 hours |
| Week 3 | Read Chapter 10. Attempt Exercises 10.1 and 10.3, then the short capstone task we send you | About 2 hours |
| At the end | Answer part B of the knowledge check, then a 60-minute conversation | 1 hour 15 minutes |

The other exercises in each chapter are optional. The whole book is drafted; read other chapters if you wish, but the questions are about Chapters 1, 3 and 10.

The book is online at <https://aarontaycheehsien.github.io/search-for-evidence-synthesis/>.

## How to attempt the exercises

1. Write your answer before opening the worked answer. The worked answers are collapsed under each exercise.
2. Then compare. Note whether your answer matched, partly matched or did not, and what you missed or did differently.
3. If you think the worked answer is wrong or incomplete, say so. That is a finding, not a failure.

Every exercise's inputs are in the chapter or in the [case pack](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/search-for-evidence-synthesis/tree/main/case-pack). You do not need to run any code; the tables and files you need are linked from each exercise.

## How to give feedback

After each chapter, fill in the **Pilot feedback** form:

- from the book, use "Report an issue" in the right-hand margin of any page, then choose **Pilot feedback**; or
- go to the repository's [new issue page](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/search-for-evidence-synthesis/issues/new/choose).

Issues are public. If you would rather not post publicly, email your answers to the author instead; the form's questions are listed below so you can answer them by email.

If you find something wrong at any point (a fact, a number, a citation, a product claim that is out of date), use the **Error report** form.

### The questions in the Pilot feedback form

1. Which chapter is this about?
2. Roughly how long did you spend reading, and how long on the exercises?
3. For each core exercise, did your answer match the worked answer, partly match, or not match? What did you miss or do differently?
4. Which sections were unclear, and why? Quote a phrase or give the section heading.
5. Which sections were longer than they needed to be, or could be cut?
6. What is missing that you expected?
7. How much detail about how search systems work did the chapter give? (Far too little; a little too little; about right; a little too much; far too much.)
8. What, if anything, will you do differently in your work after reading it?
9. Anything else.

## What happens to your feedback

The author logs each point in the repository's pilot findings, without your name, and decides on a change for each one. You will be told what changed. If you would like to be thanked by name in the book, say so; nobody is named without their agreement.
