**Subject:** Would you test three chapters of a draft book on search for evidence synthesis?

Dear [name],

I am writing an open textbook for evidence-synthesis librarians, *How Search Finds and Misses Studies: Information Retrieval for Evidence Synthesis*. It explains how databases and newer AI tools decide which records a review team ever sees, so that readers can work out where an eligible study was lost and judge a search tool against the claim actually made for it. The draft is online at <https://aarontaycheehsien.github.io/search-for-evidence-synthesis/>.

Before revising it, I want to find out where it fails for practising librarians. I am inviting five to eight people who have built at least one search for a systematic or scoping review in the last two years, and I would value your view [say why: for example, your work on a particular kind of review or field].

**What I would ask of you**

- About five hours over three weeks: read Chapters 1, 3 and 10, and attempt two short exercises in each before opening the worked answers.
- Two 15-minute knowledge checks, one before you start and one at the end.
- A 60-minute conversation at the end, on its own or with one or two other participants.

I would hope to start on [date] and finish by [date]. The attached guide gives the details.

**How your feedback is used**

You can give feedback through a short form on the book's GitHub repository, which is public, or by email to me if you prefer. I record each point without your name and decide on a change for each one, and I will tell you what changed. If you would like to be thanked by name in the book, you can say so; nobody is named without agreement. This is feedback on a draft text, not research on people, but if your institution needs to approve this kind of activity, please check before agreeing.

The pilot is unpaid. [Optional: say what you can offer, such as an acknowledgement or early notice of the first edition.]

Could you let me know by [date] whether you can take part? I am happy to answer questions first, and it is entirely fine to say no.
